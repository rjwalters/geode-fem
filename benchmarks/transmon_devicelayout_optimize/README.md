# `benchmarks/transmon_devicelayout_optimize/` — DeviceLayout.jl SingleTransmon re-optimization (#1092 part 2)

DeviceLayout.jl's SingleTransmon example tunes two parameters, the transmon
`capacitor_length` and the readout resonator `total_length`, toward
`targets_GHz = [3.0, 4.0]` (`run_optimization` in the example source; the
blog does not state the targets). It uses PRIMA (derivative-free) in the
variables `x1 = √(620 µm / c)`, `x2 = 5000 µm / L`, with box `[0.6, 1.4]²`.
It stops when `Σ (f − t)² / t² / 2 < 1e-4` and reports 9 Palace runs to
`c = 1217.678 µm`, `L = 7243.955 µm` at `[3.005, 3.982]` GHz.

This benchmark repeats that optimization with geode's gradient, against two
objectives:

1. **DeviceLayout's own discretization.** This is the comparison with PRIMA:
   Palace on DeviceLayout's mesh_order 2 mesh at solver_order 2, with the
   example's `configfile` verbatim.
2. **The converged frequencies** (part 1 shows DeviceLayout's discretization
   sits 6–8 % below them on the qubit).

## Method

- **`capacitor_length`: shape gradient.**
  - **The motion.** DeviceLayout keeps the junction at the origin and builds
    the island up from y = 12 µm. Everything above the island translates
    rigidly with c, and the box re-centers by c/2 (checked on the part-1
    meshes). `examples/transmon_devicelayout_optimize.rs` turns that motion
    into a node velocity ∂X/∂c.
  - **The mesh check.** Before using the velocity, the example verifies on
    the mesh that every metal outline node in a stretched or blended band
    lies on an edge the motion maps onto itself.
  - **The gradient.** The adjoint-free Hellmann–Feynman contraction
    `EigenSensitivity::deigenvalue_dtheta_tensor_eps` (new; sapphire tensor
    through the exact full-tensor element JVP) takes 0.4 s per solve. On the
    initial mesh it matches a central difference of two re-solves of the
    moved mesh to every printed digit.
- **`total_length`: transmission line.** DeviceLayout re-lays the meander when
  L changes, so no fixed-topology node motion represents it.
  - f_r (L + δ) is constant, with δ = 216 µm from one finite difference of
    two DeviceLayout meshes (L = 5000 → 5250 µm).
  - δ is weakly determined. Remesh noise in f_r is about 0.15 %, which is
    roughly ±150 µm in δ from one 250 µm step. A second point, Palace
    dl1 → dl2 corrected for the change in c, gives about 363 µm (about 2 %
    in L + δ).
  - ∂f_q/∂L = 0. The dressing shift through the detuning is not measured
    here; it is small next to the remesh noise.
  - δ and ∂f_q/∂L enter only the L column of the Newton Jacobian. Every
    design is evaluated on the true objective, so neither enters the
    designs themselves; a poorer Jacobian can only cost extra steps.
- **Why Newton runs on the high-fidelity objective.** DeviceLayout re-meshes
  every design, so the order-1 objective is not smooth in the parameters. A
  30 µm change of c moves f_q by more than twice what the fixed-topology
  derivative predicts, and a 250 µm change of L moves f_q by 1.4 %.
  - So there is no inner optimization on the cheap solves.
  - Newton runs directly on the high-fidelity objective, in DeviceLayout's
    variables x1, x2. Its Jacobian is geode's gradient scaled by the
    measured fidelity ratio S = F / f_h at the same design.
  - Each step costs one geode order-1 solve with its gradient (19–25 s in
    the records) and one high-fidelity evaluation.
  - A one-time δ calibration (one extra DeviceLayout mesh at L = 5250 µm and
    its geode solve, `runs/calibL_5250.toml`) is setup cost and is not
    counted in the per-step cost below.

## Files

- `results.toml`: the artifact (MANIFEST bucket A), written by
  `cargo run -p geode-core --release --example transmon_devicelayout_optimize`
  (assemble mode). Pinned by `tests/transmon_devicelayout_optimize.rs`.
- `runs/`: geode solve records with gradients, one per evaluation. They are
  written by the same example in solve mode on the worker, via `driver.py`.
- `opt_log.jsonl`: the driver's log of every evaluation and step.
- `driver.py`, `ladder.sh`, `worker_ladder.sh`: the orchestration. Meshes are
  generated by DeviceLayout on the host with Julia, and the solves run on the
  AWS worker.
- `ladder_<tag>/`: geode records, logs and mesh sha256 of the evaluation
  ladders.
- `logs/`, `reports/`: solve logs, `time -v` records, per-design generator
  reports and mesh sha256.
- Palace cells: `reference/fixtures/transmon_palace/results_optimize_1092/`.

## Results

### Against DeviceLayout's discretization (the comparison with PRIMA)

| step | capacitor_length (µm) | total_length (µm) | Palace at DL discretization (GHz) | err |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 (initial, part-1 run) | 620.000 | 5000.000 | 4.1683, 5.6132 | 1.6e-1 |
| 1 | 1338.338 | 7137.638 | 2.8859, 4.0465 | 7.9e-4 |
| 2 | 1225.346 | 7223.250 | 3.0120, 4.0009 | 8.0e-6 |
| 3 | 1236.360 | 7224.900 | 3.0013, 4.0011 | 1.4e-7 |

- **Cost.** The run meets DeviceLayout's criterion (err < 1e-4) at step 2.
  That took **3 Palace runs**, counting the initial one as DeviceLayout does,
  against PRIMA's 9, plus 3 geode order-1 solves with gradients at 19–25 s
  each. Each Palace run took about 4 min on 32 ranks. The one-time δ
  calibration (one extra mesh and geode solve) is excluded from that count.
- **The optimum.** Step 3 reaches err 1.4e-7 at (1236.360, 7224.900) µm,
  which is +18.7 / −19.1 µm from DeviceLayout's published
  (1217.678, 7243.955).
  - Part of the c offset is the Palace build. This build gives
    3.0164 / 3.9956 GHz at the published final, against the published
    3.005 / 3.982, which explains about +10 µm of c.
  - The rest is where PRIMA stopped. Its published pair sits 0.17 % / 0.45 %
    off the targets, inside its 1 % stopping rule.

### Against the converged frequencies

Part 1 puts DeviceLayout's published final design at 3.265 / 4.088 GHz
converged. Newton steps from that design use the part-1 ladder and the geode
gradient there. Each new design is evaluated by a full ladder: Palace order 1
at h, h/2, h/4 and order 2 at h, h/2, plus geode at h, h/2. The converged
values are part-1 estimates with their band: the point estimate is the
order-1 extrapolation, and the band spans the eigenvalue-variable and
observed-order extrapolations of the same cells. The band does not include
mesh-family spread: every ladder is one DeviceLayout mesh family refined.

| design | capacitor_length (µm) | total_length (µm) | converged estimate [band] (GHz) | estimate vs targets |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| published final | 1217.678 | 7243.955 | 3.2652 [3.236, 3.471], 4.0880 [4.077, 4.139] | +8.8 %, +2.2 % |
| cv1 | 1481.657 | 7407.945 | 2.9672 [2.938, 3.188], 4.0044 [3.993, 4.055] | −1.09 %, +0.11 % |
| cv2 | 1446.078 | 7416.352 | 3.0047 [2.979, 3.221], 3.9984 [3.987, 4.067] | +0.16 %, −0.04 % |

- **The result.** The converged design needs a substantially longer
  capacitor than DeviceLayout's published final design: about +16 % to
  +38 % (point estimate +19 %, +228 µm), with a resonator about 2.4 % longer
  (+172 µm).
  - **The band.** At cv2 both targets lie inside the band: qubit
    [2.979, 3.221] GHz (−0.9 % / +7.4 % around the estimate), resonator
    [3.987, 4.067] GHz. With dF/dc ≈ S·∂f_h/∂c ≈ −9e-4 GHz/µm there, the
    qubit band maps to roughly c ∈ [1418, 1685] µm.
  - **The estimate leans low.** The order-1 (h/2, h/4) and order-2
    (h, h/2) series share a limit at order 0.87 (qubit) and 0.84
    (resonator). That shared limit puts cv2 at 3.039 / 4.019 GHz, +1.3 % /
    +0.5 % over the targets, so the qubit is outside DeviceLayout's 1 %
    reading on that check. The converged design is therefore probably at or
    above the point-estimate c.
  - So cv2 lands on the targets within the part-1 band; it is not a
    verified hit of 3.0 / 4.0 GHz converged.
- **Cost.** Two Newton steps, each with one geode gradient solve at h and one
  evaluation ladder (about 1.7 h of worker time per ladder, dominated by
  the order-2 h/2 cell).
- **Same-mesh agreement.** geode and Palace agree to 2e-4 % on the qubit at
  both levels of each ladder. The resonator agrees to 0.027 % at h and
  0.0000 % at h/2, for the reason given in the part-1 README: Palace
  bisects the h mesh.

## Notes

- **An aborted first attempt.** The first DeviceLayout-discretization run
  took Newton steps in c and L directly. It was stopped before its first
  Palace run finished, and restarted in DeviceLayout's variables x1, x2, in
  which the frequencies are close to linear. Its single geode record was
  discarded.
- **Ladder wall times are records, not benchmarks.** The worker ran one
  cell at a time, but mesh uploads and the other phase's geode solves
  sometimes overlapped.
