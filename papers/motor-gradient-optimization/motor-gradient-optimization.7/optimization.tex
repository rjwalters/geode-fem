\section{Matched optimization study}\label{sec:optimization}
The preceding sections establish that the gradients are accurate for the
model they differentiate. This section tests whether they shorten the time
needed to reach a common validated ripple target. The protocol, its
amendments and every later deviation are recorded in the
\href{https://github.com/rjwalters/geode-fem/issues/1185}{preregistration}
and were fixed before any main-run outcome was observed.

\subsection{Protocol}
\paragraph{Methods.}
Five methods form an optimizer-by-resolution comparison plus one
surrogate (Table~\ref{tab:methods}). A and E are safeguarded SLSQP \citep{kraft1988} using
the field adjoints of Section~\ref{sec:gradients}; B and D are COBYQA
\citep{ragonneau2022}, both through SciPy 1.18.1 \citep{virtanen2020scipy}, with $2n+1$ interpolation points and unit-box trust
radii $0.1$ to $10^{-6}$; C is an empirical cubic radial-basis trust-region
surrogate fitted to L1 evaluations. A/B and E/D vary the optimizer at fixed
training resolution, and A/E and B/D vary resolution at fixed optimizer.
The optimizer contrast includes differences in search strategy, so it does
not isolate gradient access as the only causal difference. Training uses
order-45 coupling torque on 20 angles at current fractions $0$, $\tfrac12$
and $1$ of the fixed peak current $9.6777\,\mathrm{A}$; L0 and L1 differ only
in spatial resolution, and neither is fitted or corrected against validation.

\paragraph{Design spaces and constraints.}
Space A has eight free geometric lengths: MAG, MH, MW, SW1--SW4 and Sr1
(Appendix~\ref{sec:parameters}), that is, the rotor iron depth, magnet
height and width, four stator tooth and slot dimensions, and the slot-bottom
fillet radius. Space B adds ten grouped
offsets acting on 55 surface control points. Stack length, radial scale,
stator outer diameter and operating angle are fixed, so the saved Opt120
design lies outside both spaces. The two spaces also use different discrete
mesh-motion maps, so their contrast does not isolate the ten added offsets.
The objective is $F=\sum_c\sigma_c^2$ over the three training currents,
subject to a full-current mean of at least $1.0025\,\mu_0$ and normalized
construction margins $c_i-0.05\geq0$. Unbuildable or mesh-infeasible
candidates are refused: SLSQP restores toward the last admissible point and
restarts, COBYQA receives a NaN barrier, and C skips the point. A moved mesh
with an inverted triangle or a minimum angle below $5^\circ$ is repaired by
up to four charged remeshing attempts.

\paragraph{Common validation and credit.}
Every method is judged on the same order-93, L2, 20-angle validation model.
Its initial-design anchor is $F_0=9.6034\times10^{-3}\,(\Nm)^2$ and
$\mu_0=1.44084\,\Nm$. The targets are $T_{50}=0.5F_0$, $T_{90}=0.1F_0$ and
$T_{99}=0.01F_0$. A validated design attains a target only if
\begin{equation}
 F_{\mathrm{val}}+\delta F\leq T,\qquad
 \delta F=\max\{0.10F_{\mathrm{val}},\,2.5\times10^{-7}\,(\Nm)^2\},
\end{equation}
its full-current mean meets the floor, its construction margins and mesh
checks pass, and its validation completes within budget. The allowance
$\delta F$ is an empirical margin, not a bound on continuum error. Optimizer
success flags confer no credit. Changed incumbents are validated as they
appear, after the initial interpolation set for B, C and D.

\paragraph{Budget and timing.}
Cost is wall time, in units of the median cold validation forward measured
on the same host at the same concurrency. Each run receives 120 units,
with a 60-unit checkpoint that counts only validations completed by then.
The charge covers evaluator start-up, geometry checks, mesh motion and
repair, training solves, adjoints, optimizer overhead, surrogate fitting
and validation; nothing is subtracted as overhead. L0 receives no discount.

\paragraph{Starts and execution.}
Each space uses $\theta_0$ and four Latin-hypercube starts (seeds 1--4),
with the scalar coordinates matched across spaces. All starts were frozen
before any main outcome. Every method runs from every start twice, giving
100 runs. Within a start, repeat 1 rotates the method order and repeat 2
reverses it. Because each optimizer is deterministic, the two repeats
follow the same path and measure timing noise only. The comparison is
therefore a paired case study over five starts, not a sample of
independent replicates.

The session ran on one AWS c7a.4xlarge host (x86-64, OpenBLAS) with 15
concurrent single-threaded processes. Before calibration, the host
recomputed the initial-design validation waveforms in both spaces; they
agreed with the canonical anchor within the preregistered $10^{-9}$
tolerances, and the two spaces agreed bit for bit. The calibrated unit was
$70.94\,\mathrm{s}$, the median of 45 concurrent cold validations. All 100
runs completed in one session with no recovery, deferral or contamination:
the one-minute load average peaked at 21.1, below the contamination
threshold of $1.5N=22.5$. The in-run cost totals
109~CPU-hours.

\subsection{Results}
\paragraph{Attained quality.}
Figure~\ref{fig:exp2-quality} shows the best credited value of
$(F+\delta F)/F_0$ for every start, method and space (repeat~1).
In space A, no run goes below $F/F_0=0.043$ and none reaches $T_{99}$.
The best designs of A, B, D and E all reach $0.043F_0$, and C's
reaches $0.049F_0$. Because the floor is shared by both optimizers and both
training resolutions, it reflects the eight-parameter space at this
validation model rather than any one method. Starts matter more than
methods there: from LHS~4, A and B stop near $0.09F_0$, D reaches
$0.044F_0$, and C and E never produce a credited validation, in either
repeat. In space B, each method's best design reaches $0.004$--$0.009F_0$,
and every method except C reaches $T_{99}$ from at least four of five
starts.

\paragraph{Time to target.}
Table~\ref{tab:exp2-time} and Figure~\ref{fig:exp2-time} give the charged
time to each target, per start. The two repeats agree closely: over the
111 target crossings attained in both repeats, the median difference is
2.1\% and the largest is 13.2\%. Repeat-1 times are reported. Only six
crossings occur after the 60-unit checkpoint, all at $T_{99}$ in space B
and all for B or D.

Table~\ref{tab:exp2-ratios} gives paired time ratios. A ratio is formed
only when both methods attain the target from that start; no finite ratio
is derived from a censored cell. In both spaces and at every attained
target, gradient-based SLSQP reaches the target sooner than COBYQA at the
same training resolution. At L1 (B/A), the geometric-mean ratio is 3.1--3.3
in space A and 5.1--7.8 in space B. At L0 (D/E), it is 2.4--2.8 and
3.1--7.2. All 45 finite optimizer-contrast pairs favor SLSQP, with
per-start ratios from 1.46 to 21.5. The coarse mesh shortens the time by a
smaller factor: the geometric-mean E/A ratio is 0.62--0.79 and D/B is
0.45--0.56. Against D, the inexpensive derivative-free physical competitor,
A is faster by a geometric mean of 1.68--1.83 in space A and 2.25--4.32 in
space B. This contrast changes both factors; one of its 25 pairs is a tie
(0.99, space-A LHS~2 at $T_{90}$).

The speed of E has a reliability cost. E never produces a credited
validation from space-A LHS~4, and from space-B LHS~1 it stalls at
$0.28F_0$, missing $T_{90}$ and $T_{99}$. A, by contrast, attains every
target that any method reaches from every start. C attains $T_{99}$ only
once in space B and reaches $T_{90}$ from two of five space-A starts.

\paragraph{Final refinement.}
The preregistered selection takes, for each method and space, the
lowest-$F_{\mathrm{val}}$ feasible design over both repeats, and the
earliest repeat-1 crossing of each attained target. With $\theta_0$ in
each space and identical designs merged, this gives 33 designs. Each
received the six registered checks: the primary L2 validation, L3, a
40-angle grid, Newton tolerance $10^{-12}$, an independently solved grid
shifted by $0.5^\circ$, and held-out currents $0.25$ and $0.75$. The 15
designs with zero stator offsets also received the conforming outer-band
Arkkio check on fresh meshes. All 213 checks completed
(Table~\ref{tab:exp2-refinement}).

The checks ran after the study, on a different host (Apple M3 Ultra,
macOS arm64). For the 16 selected designs validated on the moved
reference mesh, the primary check reproduces the study's $F_{\mathrm{val}}$
within $2.1\times10^{-12}$ relative. The other 15 needed a fresh mesh,
which Gmsh triangulates differently on the two platforms; their $F$
differs by at most $1.7\times10^{-3}$ relative. This measures the
sensitivity of $F$ to remeshing and is far below the 10\% allowance
$\delta F$.

Spatial refinement is the largest change. L3 moves $F$ by $-4.0\%$ to
$+6.3\%$; the largest increase belongs to the overall best design, D's in
space B, whose $F$ rises from $0.0044F_0$ to $0.0047F_0$. Angular and
nonlinear effects are small: the 40-angle and shifted grids change $F$ by
at most $0.11\%$, and the tighter Newton tolerance by at most $10^{-5}$.
The conforming estimator gives $1.1\%$ to $4.4\%$ more ripple than the
order-93 coupling on the space-A designs, and $2.0\%$ more at $\theta_0$.
All 25 target crossings still satisfy their target, allowance included,
at L3; the smallest remaining margin is 1.2\% of the target. Every
candidate also satisfies the full-current mean floor in all five
training-current checks, the closest by 0.012\%. The refinement therefore
confirms the credited crossings and leaves the time-to-target comparison
unchanged.

The held-out currents test the ripple reduction outside the three
training currents. Relative to $\theta_0$ at the same currents, the
reduction ranges from 0.65 to 1.73 times its value at the training
currents. In space A it is within 10\% for all five best designs. In space
B, the best designs lose more: D's best design has
$0.0044F_0$ at the training currents but $0.0076$ of $\theta_0$'s value at
the held-out currents. The extra freedom of space B is therefore partly
spent on the three training currents.
