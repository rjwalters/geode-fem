\section{Prospective optimization study}
\label{sec:prospective-optimization}

\noindent\textbf{Draft status.}
This prospective section reports no optimization outcome or speedup.
Measured results, execution metadata and independent refinement checks are
required before a performance claim. The prospective rules follow the
\href{https://github.com/rjwalters/geode-fem/issues/1185}{preregistered protocol}
and its adopted amendments; protocol choices are fixed before main outcomes.

\subsection{Question and comparison}

The study will test whether exact discrete field gradients reduce the cost of
reaching a common validated torque-ripple target, and whether that result
changes when additional shape freedom is available. A coarse nonlinear FEM
model provides the inexpensive physical competitor. Comparing both optimizers
at both training resolutions separates optimizer choice from spatial
resolution within this experiment (Table~\ref{tab:planned-methods}). The
optimizer comparison includes differences in search strategy; it does not
isolate gradient access as the only causal difference. The matched contrasts
are A/B and E/D for optimizer choice at fixed resolution, and A/E and B/D
for resolution at fixed optimizer. A/D changes both factors.

Within each optimizer, all options, stopping and callback rules will be
identical across training resolutions. SLSQP uses the field adjoints and
$\mathrm{ftol}=10^{-15}$; COBYQA uses SciPy 1.18.1, $2n+1$ initial interpolation
points, and initial/final unit-box trust radii $0.1$ and $10^{-6}$, where
$n$ is the number of free parameters (8 or 18).
The safeguards below apply equally to A/E and B/D.

Both training levels will use coupling torque, harmonic maximum $h=45$, twenty rotor angles,
the same nonlinear material law, and current fractions
$\mathcal C=\{0,\tfrac12,1\}$ of the fixed peak current $9.6777\,\mathrm{A}$.
Only spatial resolution changes between L0 and L1. Each level will have its own
initial-design training normalization, measured before the optimization runs.
The normalized training mean floors can therefore correspond to different
physical torques; common validation, rather than either training model,
decides feasibility. There is no fitted or bias correction, cross-level
gradient substitution, adaptive refinement, or tuning of L0 against validation
outputs. Any later change requires a prospective protocol amendment.

Method C linearly interpolates predicted standard deviation at $0.25$ or
$0.75$ between its neighboring modeled currents, without a nonlinear solve; extra mean RBFs at $0$ and $0.5$ use the same training data after
the run and cannot affect its path. C's diagnostic uses its final incumbent
and compares predictions with both L1 and common validation. Its checks at
current $0.5$ test new designs and a finer model, not an unseen current;
the withheld-current checks measure this specified interpolation rule.

For D and E, each stopped run will select the feasible point with the lowest
common-validation objective, with the earliest validation breaking ties. If none exists,
the last buildable training incumbent will be used with an explicit lack of
validated feasibility; if that is also missing, the diagnostic is unavailable.
At this frozen point, L0 and the common validation model will solve the
nonlinear equations at fractions $0.25$ and $0.75$ on twenty angles, retaining
waveforms, means and standard deviations. These checks will not fit a correction
or modify the stopped optimization. Separately, every D/E validation will
retain paired L0 and common-validation objective, all three current-specific
standard deviations, and full-current mean, including validations that fail
acceptance. The fidelity assessment will use all these records, not only the
winning designs.

Before main optimization, L0 adjoints for both the scalar and grouped-control
spaces will be checked at frozen admissible designs against directional
central differences over a step-size sweep. Failed checks will remain in the
record; their costs belong to setup, not optimizer timing. A mixed-resolution
smoke must also verify that changing L0/L1 training leaves the common L2
validator and exact anchor identity unchanged. Smoke is wiring evidence only.

\subsection{Design spaces, objective and feasibility}

Space A has eight free engineering parameters. Space B contains the same
scalars and ten grouped surface-control offsets acting on 55 control points;
the points are not independent design variables. The spaces also use different
discrete mesh-motion conventions: Jacobian-stiffened extension for the scalar
map and plain harmonic extension for the control-point map, with different
boundary parameter conventions. Space comparisons therefore do not isolate
the effect of the ten extra offsets. Within each space, all methods share
the same motion scheme. Axial length, overall radial
scale, stator diameter and operating-angle convention remain fixed at
$0.1\,\mathrm{m}$, $1$, $0.135\,\mathrm{m}$ and $0^\circ$, respectively.
The historical Opt120 design is a construction-regression reference outside
this fixed-size problem; the new domain will not be relaxed to admit it. The
parameter box bounds magnet area but does not hold magnet volume constant.
The continuous-geometry gap and outer-web bounds established separately do
not constitute manufacturing qualification or a guarantee for every mesh.
The separately reviewed bounds are magnet area $20$--$300\,\mathrm{mm}^2$
($133\,\mathrm{mm}^2$ initially), continuous gap greater than
$0.32876\,\mathrm{mm}$, and outer web at least $0.5\,\mathrm{mm}$.
[PENDING supplement reference for exact parameter/group definitions,
boxes and the geometry-bound derivation]

Let $\sigma_{r,c}(\theta)$ and $\mu_{r,c}(\theta)$ denote torque standard
deviation and mean at design $\theta$, model $r$, and current fraction $c$.
The training objective and full-current constraint are
\begin{align}
 F_r(\theta) &= \sum_{c\in\mathcal C}\sigma_{r,c}(\theta)^2,
 & \mu_{r,1}(\theta)&\geq 1.0025\,\mu_{r,1}(\theta_0).
\end{align}
Optimization uses unit-box coordinates and objective/mean normalization by
the corresponding level's initial-design values. Only the full-current mean
is constrained. Half-current mean torque will be reported as a diagnostic;
this protocol supplies no partial-load mean-torque guarantee.

Construction margins $m_i$ have fixed positive scales $s_i$ of matching units.
For a nonzero reference margin, $s_i=|m_i(\theta_0)|$; a zero reference margin
requires an individually justified fixed scale. Length and squared-length
margins, including the arc discriminant, keep their respective dimensions.
The additional normalized constraints are
\begin{equation}
 c_i(\theta)=m_i(\theta)/s_i,\qquad
 h_i(\theta)=c_i(\theta)-0.05\geq0.
\end{equation}
The construction's actual strict and non-strict guards and its discrete
checks determine whether a trial is buildable. Buildable trials can still
violate $h_i\geq0$: such trials are evaluated normally and are constraint
infeasible; this alone does not trigger restoration. The value $0.05$ is a
prospective domain margin, not a certified physical safety factor, and will
not be tuned on optimizer outcomes. Guards are evaluated before unsafe square
roots or inverse trigonometric operations, retaining raw margins and defined
downstream refusal information. A zero arc discriminant can permit a value
while its design derivative is undefined; no gradient is invented there.
For target credit and best-feasible selection only, every scaled margin must
satisfy $h_i\geq-10^{-12}$, allowing rounding at active constraints.
Optimizer constraints remain $h_i\geq0$; buildability retains its exact guards
without a rounding tolerance.
Geometry Jacobians use analytic expressions or geometry-only central
differences, with their checks and costs recorded separately from field solves.
An undefined construction or derivative will be rejected before the
corresponding field request. No request may return another design's value,
gradient or constraint, including after restoration.
[PENDING frozen smooth/discrete guard and scale inventory, implied physical
floors, exclusion records and derivative/regression evidence]

SLSQP will abort a call that requests a refused point, restore toward the last
admissible evaluated point by at most twenty bisections, and restart at the
actual restored point with fresh optimizer state. At most twenty restarts
are allowed. Geometric restoration tests points on the segment from that
anchor toward the rejected design; if no admissible anchor/probe exists,
the run ends as \texttt{restoration\_failed}. Mesh-infeasible requests also
require the anchor and restored mesh to pass the mesh policy, with mesh work
charged. Rejected and restored coordinates, counts and costs remain in the
trace. COBYQA will use the pinned NaN barrier. The empirical surrogate
will skip refused samples without fitting them. An initial coordinate step
tries the opposite sign after refusal, then at most three halvings in both
directions; failure to form a full-rank set ends as
\texttt{restoration\_failed}. Geometry and subproblem points obey the same
buildability checks, and the surrogate subproblem includes $h\geq0$.
Its feasible-first ordering must include
the geometry constraints as well as the mean constraint.

Any inverted moved triangle or a minimum angle below $5^\circ$ triggers mesh
repair. Fresh meshes must meet the same quality policy. At most four attempts
are permitted, each beginning from defaults: default settings;
\texttt{Mesh.Algorithm=5} (Delaunay), or algorithm 1 (MeshAdapt) if the default is
already 5; characteristic-length
factor $0.9$; then factor $0.8$. Every attempt, refusal and restoration is
recorded and charged, including the SHA-256 of every training remesh.
The actual algorithm is recorded, and a fixture must establish that the
second attempt changes mesh bytes. All-four failure is candidate-dependent
mesh infeasibility and invokes the method's refusal mechanism.
The $5^\circ$ floor also imposes an implicit geometric constraint: refinement
cannot remove a limiting geometric corner angle (a development fixture
remained at $4.017^\circ$ on all four attempts). Space B's slot-opening
channel wall is unguarded and can narrow to $0.16\,\mu\mathrm{m}$ at
minimum SW3 and maximum CPS1. This known meshing risk is handled by repair
and mesh-infeasible refusal; the protocol adds no wall-thickness constraint.
Real solver or infrastructure errors are a
separate failed-run outcome. A request in flight at budget exhaustion is
aborted with its process group, its remaining budget is charged, and its
requested coordinates and category are retained. Ordinary scientific
failures or convergence do not receive selective extra restarts.

\subsection{Common validation and target credit}

Every method will use the same L2/$h93$/g20 validation model and initial-design
anchor. Write its initial objective as $F_0$ and its full-current mean as
$\mu_0$. For a completed candidate validation, define
\begin{equation}
 \delta F(\theta)=\max\left\{0.10F_{\mathrm{val}}(\theta),
                    2.5\times10^{-7}\,(\mathrm{N\,m})^2\right\}.
\end{equation}
The three fixed targets are $T_{50}=0.5F_0$, $T_{90}=0.1F_0$, and
$T_{99}=0.01F_0$. A target is attained only when
\begin{equation}
 F_{\mathrm{val}}(\theta)+\delta F(\theta)\leq T,
 \qquad \mu_{\mathrm{val},1}(\theta)\geq1.0025\mu_0,
\end{equation}
$h_i\geq-10^{-12}$ and every discrete guard and validation-mesh policy check pass,
and validation finishes within budget. Each validation cold-starts and
depends only on its candidate; training-model infeasibility does not prevent
an otherwise eligible incumbent from being validated. The allowance is an
empirical validation rule, not
a rigorous bound on continuum error. Optimizer success flags confer no credit.
The callback rules validate changed incumbents, excluding the start and
duplicate validations. B/D complete $2n+1$ initial evaluations and C completes
$n+1$ before the non-start incumbent is validated once. The final point is
also validated if it is neither the start nor previously validated.

Each run will receive 120 measured cold-validation wall-time units, with a
60-unit checkpoint using only validations completed by that checkpoint.
Both training resolutions share the same validation timing unit; L0 costs
will be measured without a nominal resolution discount. A run that later
fails retains its earlier eligible target crossings. Failed, deferred,
contaminated and budget-censored outcomes will remain visible. Curves extend
to 120 units, and pilot trajectories are never appended to main runs.
There is no reuse of another method's training cache or trajectory; within-run
cache policies are identical across the comparison.

\subsection{Starts, scheduling and cost}

The planned cohort is $\theta_0$ plus four seeded starts in each space, five
methods and two timing repeats: 100 optimization runs. Each seed uses the
registered ten-point, eighteen-dimensional SplitMix64 Latin-hypercube
sequence, with seeds 1 through 4.
The scalar coordinates are matched across
spaces. The frozen pilot indices 1 and 4 are tried first for seeds 1 and 2;
deterministic next eligible replacements and the first eligible indices for
seeds 3 and 4 are selected without inspecting objective or method outcomes.
Every attempted index and rejection reason will be archived, and all selected
vectors will be committed bit for bit before main outcomes.
A start must pass admissibility/buildability checks and the moved-reference-mesh
policy without remeshing in both spaces at L0 and L1: zero inverted triangles
and minimum angle at least $5^\circ$. This preflight uses geometry checks and
mesh moves, with no field solve, and its costs are recorded outside run
budgets. This favors moderate
deformations reachable by mesh motion. The starts are not uniform samples of
the admissible domain, although subsequent optimizer iterates may use repairs.
The comparison is a paired case study over five designs; timing repeats are
not independent design samples.

For start index $s$, repeat 1 will rotate the method order ABCDE left by $s$;
repeat 2 will reverse that exact order. With concurrent workers, this is the
queue-entry order. The actual dispatch/completion order and worker count will
be retained. The host gate and load monitor apply to the common calibration
and to optimization runs. Calibration uses $N$ simultaneous single-threaded
processes on the same host and at the same concurrency as optimization.
Each makes three cold initial-design validation evaluations; the common unit
is the median of all $3N$ timings. The single-process median is retained as a
diagnostic, not used as the session unit. L0 and L1 reference computations
are recorded separately without introducing a second validation-time unit.
Reference constants and common calibration define the session and sit outside
individual run budgets; their costs remain in the end-to-end totals.
All methods use the same thread limits. Post-run diagnostics have a separate
interval and cost record. A run will not launch after a failed idle gate; contaminated runs
will retain their records and receive at most one contamination rerun at the
session's end. The pre-run
one-minute load average must be at most $N+1$; load above $1.5N$ continuously
for more than 60 seconds during a run marks it contaminated. The session is
eligible as idle-host evidence only if no run is contaminated or every such
run has a clean replacement. Calibration must be clean before dispatch.

Recovery preserves the first clean completed attempt for each logical job,
including scientific failures and censored outcomes. Recovered never-started
or deferred jobs and verified external-interruption replacements run after
the remaining registered queue, in their original relative order. Each job
may receive at most one whole-run replacement for a verified external
interruption, after the old processes are proved stopped. This is a new
attempt with the full registered budget, never a continued trajectory.
The source, environment, input hashes, settings, host, worker count and
recorded calibration unit must match; recovery never remeasures that unit.
Unknown interruptions remain unresolved. Solver failures, mesh infeasibility,
safeguard stops and target misses do not permit replacements, and recovery
does not reset the separate contamination allowance. An interrupted post-run
diagnostic is repaired independently, without repeating completed optimization.
All original records and partial costs are retained, with incomplete accounting
identified. The report will list every replacement, reason, supporting evidence
and original partial cost, and count the affected jobs. These are adopted
rules; recovery execution still requires implementation and review.

The charged run ledger includes evaluator startup, geometry checks, mesh
updates and repairs, assembly and solves, adjoints, search overhead,
surrogate fitting and validation. Shared preparation/calibration costs and
post-run costs will also be reported, together with end-to-end execution
costs, split by resolution and into shared versus method-specific work.
Raw seconds, normalized wall-time units, solve counts and measured secondary
solve-count weights will all be retained; only wall time stops a run.
CPU user/system seconds will be sampled inside each evaluator request and
inside each isolated run process. A run includes its own CPU and its reaped
children once; nested request totals are a cross-check, never added again.
Killed or unreaped descendants leave accounting explicitly incomplete.
A child-specific exit receipt provides an independent inclusive cross-check.
RSS observations will identify their process scope and cumulative high-water
semantics; separate process maxima will not be summed into a purported
simultaneous process-tree peak. Raw platform units and unavailable metrics
will be explicit. [PENDING main-run host, process count,
environment versions, calibration records and source/data hashes]

\subsection{Final checks and interpretation}

For each method and space, the best-observed representative will be the
lowest-$F_{\mathrm{val}}$ eligible feasible validation from uncontaminated
main runs. Feasibility here requires the common full-current mean floor,
$h_i\geq-10^{-12}$, all discrete and validation-mesh checks, and validation completion
within budget. Ties are broken by start
index, repeat index and validation ordinal. Earlier eligible records of
failed runs remain candidates. A missing feasible representative will be
reported explicitly; any optional buildable-only diagnostic receives no
feasibility or target credit. Every considered validation and its selection
or rejection reason will be retained. For each method, space and target, the earliest attaining
validation across starts in repeat 1, measured by cumulative common
wall-time units and with the same tie-break, will also be selected: at most thirty
crossing designs in addition to ten representatives. A missing repeat-1
crossing will not be replaced with one from repeat 2.

The initial design and these selected points will be checked at L3/$h93$/g20
and L2/$h93$/g40, retaining the training-current waveforms and the specified
half-current and $0.25/0.75$ diagnostics. Each final candidate's half-current
mean will be compared with the initial design. Conforming outer-band Arkkio checks
are restricted to fresh meshes with zero stator offsets and supported
rotations. For other designs the missing conforming check and resulting
model dependence will be stated. Exact geometry/settings identities may
share a check while retaining every source role. A failed refinement will
not trigger substitution of a different selected candidate.

At all three training currents, the same initial design and deduplicated
cohort will also receive two L2/$h93$ checks: g20 with Newton relative
tolerance $10^{-12}$ against the default $10^{-8}$, and an independently
solved grid $0.5^\circ,1.5^\circ,\ldots,19.5^\circ$ at the default tolerance.
Each current sweep starts cold; successive angles use continuation from
the preceding solution. Signed waveform, mean, standard-deviation and
objective changes, together with failures, will be retained. Each candidate
will be compared with the initial design at matching settings. These checks
are charged separately after the run and neither replace candidates nor
change common-validator target credit. The even/odd samples of g40 may
additionally diagnose angular sampling without further solves; their shared
sweep history prevents treating them as an independent shifted-grid solve.

Time-to-target statements will refer to common L2/$h93$/g20 validation.
The selected refinement checks will qualify those statements where they
disagree; they do not certify every crossing or typical-start behavior.
Paired timing comparisons will retain censored targets and avoid a finite
speedup ratio when a comparator does not attain the target. Conclusions will
be confined to the tested optimizers, physical models, design spaces and
sampling conditions. Population methods such as CMA-ES remain untested, and
differences between spaces A and B will not be described as a measurement of
intrinsic effective dimension.


\subsection{Reproducibility prerequisites}

mesh/anchor hashes, queue order and commands. Runs must retain model identity
and requested/actual design bits. A supplement will bind the complete
surrogate algorithm, guard scales and refusal rules to reviewed code. The
runbook must demonstrate clean calibration and durable recovery without
resetting budgets, overwriting attempts or mixing calibrations. Main execution
requires a reviewed merged implementation and the selected host.
